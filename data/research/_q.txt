Is trust changing in the AI era, and is AI actually a cause? Examine trust along five distinct axes: (1) interpersonal trust between people, (2) trust in AI systems themselves, (3) trust in media and information, (4) trust in politicians and institutions, and (5) trust within workplaces, including between employers and employees and in AI-mediated hiring and monitoring.

Prioritise recent peer-reviewed academic research from roughly 2023 to 2026, and large longitudinal survey programmes that measure the same question over many years, so that change over time can actually be established. Relevant long-running instruments include the Edelman Trust Barometer, the OECD Trust Survey, Pew Research Center, Eurobarometer, the World Values Survey, the Reuters Institute Digital News Report, Gallup, and the KPMG and University of Melbourne global study of trust in AI. Report actual figures and the size of any change, not adjectives, and give the sample size, the countries covered and the year for every figure.

The central analytical question, which matters more than any single number: trust in media, politicians and institutions was already declining in many countries for decades before generative AI existed. So distinguish carefully between (a) a pre-existing long-term trend that continues, (b) a genuine acceleration or reversal that coincides with the arrival of generative AI in late 2022, and (c) a demonstrated causal effect established by experimental or quasi-experimental work. Say explicitly which of these three any given finding supports. Treat any source that asserts AI has caused a decline in trust without separating it from the pre-existing trend as making an unsupported claim, and say so.

Also cover: whether trust in AI itself is rising or falling and how sharply it differs between countries, particularly between advanced economies and emerging ones, and between the general public and experts; whether mere awareness that AI-generated content exists reduces trust in genuine authentic content, sometimes called the liar's dividend; measured effects of deepfakes and synthetic media on political trust as opposed to speculation about them; and whether people trust AI systems more or less than they trust humans for the same task, and how that varies by domain such as medicine, hiring, journalism and justice.

Where the evidence is genuinely mixed or contested, present the disagreement rather than resolving it. Note clearly where a widely repeated claim about AI and trust rests on weak or purely correlational evidence.
